Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in San Mateo

Cooling a house also dries the air, which means the unit is constantly making water it must get rid of. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the System DownSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Clear Water From the PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Call Both TradesAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Leave the system off until it is fixed.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • Length of the leak, counted from the first drip to the day the system was stopped.
  • The area of drywall and floor covering that cannot be saved.
  • Whether mold has started and needs containment and removal.
  • The water category, because Category 2 needs more cleaning than Category 1.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • A condensate drain line clogged with algae, dust and slime from the coil.
  • A rusted, cracked or out of level drain pan that lets water escape.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Mold Around the Air Handler

Mold may appear in 24 to 48 hours on damp materials by the air handler, where the return air stream can carry its spores through the ductwork to other rooms.

Baseboards and Door Trim

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Swollen fiberboard pieces do not recover and are normally swapped for new ones.

Ceilings Under the Unit

An attic air handler leaks onto the drywall beneath it. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

The Platform and Closet Walls

Closet units often sit on a wood platform over the return air box. Leaks decay the stand and climb the closet drywall, concealed by the equipment itself.
