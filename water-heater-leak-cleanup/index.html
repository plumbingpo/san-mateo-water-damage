Water Heater Leak Cleanup in San Mateo: What to Know

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

In San Mateo, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. From an attic it comes down through insulation and ceilings. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • How many stored belongings got wet and need to be moved, dried or hauled away.
  • The amount of wall drywall, ceiling drywall or insulation that has to be removed.
  • Condition of the stand or subfloor below the heater, which may need rebuilding before the replacement is set.
  • Placement of the unit, as leaks from an attic or an inside closet run higher than those in a garage.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • An aging steel tank whose bottom is eaten through by corrosion and sediment.
  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Items Stored Near the Heater

Whatever is kept by a closet or garage heater, from keepsakes to furniture and boxes, is the first thing to get wet. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.

Heater Closet Drywall

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Garage Wall Base

The wall between the garage and the house takes water into its bottom plate and drywall from the slab, and from there it reaches the floors of the living space.

Ceilings Under an Attic Heater

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Insulation that is wet no longer insulates, and the added weight can pull the ceiling drywall down.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Extract the Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Inspect the Surrounding Area

To learn the full reach of the water, our crew meters and scans the platform, the bottoms of the walls, neighboring rooms and the ceiling below if there is one.

3

Remove Wet Material

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

4

Dry and Monitor

We keep the drying equipment going for the usual 3 to 5 days needed to dry the space. We record moisture readings and tell you when the space is ready for the new heater.
