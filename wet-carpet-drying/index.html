Wet Carpet Drying: What San Mateo Homeowners Should Know

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Up top the fibers may seem barely damp, yet the pad has filled up the way a sponge does and the subfloor under it has gotten wet as well. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. Our team in San Mateo extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Warning Signs

  • Musty, sour odor that builds whenever the door and windows stay shut
  • Carpet that has come off the tack strip, leaving loose areas or ripples
  • Wet tack strip showing through as dark, rusty streaks next to the baseboard
  • Dampness that spreads outward on its own, well after the leak was stopped
  • Baseboards that have swollen or paint that bubbles low on the wall
  • Soft or squeaky subfloor beneath the area of carpet that was soaked
Our Process

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Pull Out the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry Every Layer

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Days 3 through 7

Carpet Starts Failing

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold Under the Carpet

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Water that started clean does not stay that way. As it sits in carpet and pad it picks up soil and bacteria, so a carpet that could have been dried may have to be removed.

Swollen Subfloor

Particleboard and OSB under carpet swell and crumble when they stay wet. The soft spots, squeaking and lifted seams appear weeks afterward, when nobody remembers the spill.

Wet Wall Bottoms

Water that runs under the carpet reaches the base of the walls. It wicks up into the baseboards and drywall unnoticed, since the carpet hides that edge.
