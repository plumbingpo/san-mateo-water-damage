Wall and Drywall Water Damage in San Mateo

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

Warning Signs

  • Door casing and trim joints separating from the drywall
  • Damp, stale odor concentrated along a single wall
  • Carpet edges or floorboards wet and discolored right next to the wall
  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
  • Speckled growth showing up where furniture sits against the wall or at corners
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
What We Do

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Based on the water category, how long it sat and how the drywall feels, we tell you plainly which sections can be dried in place and which need to come out.

3

Cut or Ventilate

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Receptacles and cables sit low in the wall, right where water collects. Do not use outlets on a wet wall until the circuit has been checked.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. Drying from inside the room is not enough when the insulation is soaked.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Outside walls have sheathing past the insulation that can also get soaked. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Sections of wall covered by cabinets, a vanity or shelving stay damp because air never reaches them. Checking under the cabinet base and on the reverse face tells us if it is wet.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Starts Climbing

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

Hours 24 to 48

Swelling and Blisters

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. Within the wall the insulation sags, a smell develops and mold moves over the back of the panel.

Past one week

Damage Reaches the Studs

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.
