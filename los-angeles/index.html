How Long Drying Takes in a Los Angeles Home

A wet building left alone does dry eventually, but far too slowly. Left alone, water trapped in drywall, wood framing and subfloor can stay for weeks, which is long enough for mold and warping. On each Los Angeles job, our task is to make that happen quickly and under control.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Next, air movers placed around the room drive air over damp materials so the water in them evaporates. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and send it down a drain, which keeps the room dry enough for evaporation to continue. Air movers on their own only stir humid air, so the two machines work as a pair.

We take moisture readings every day and compare them with a dry part of the same building. For most Los Angeles homes the target is met in 3 to 5 days, though hardwood, plaster and other dense materials need more time. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.

Restoration Challenges Los Angeles Homeowners Face

Water damage does not always arrive as a sudden gush across the kitchen. Below are the situations our crews are called to most often around Los Angeles, and what each one tends to involve.

Hidden leaks: A pinhole in a supply line or a loose drain fitting can drip inside a wall for months. Clues include flooring that feels spongy, blistered paint and a higher water bill you cannot explain. Thermal imaging and moisture readings let us map how far it spread before anything gets cut open. Even a slow leak matters, because wood and drywall that stay damp will rot and grow mold.

Sewage: When a main drain line clogs, wastewater comes back up through floor drains, tubs and toilets on the lowest level. It is typically caused by roots in the line, built-up grease or a municipal sewer overwhelmed during a downpour. This is Category 3 water, which carries bacteria and should not be handled without protective gear. Carpet, padding and other absorbent materials that got wet usually cannot be saved.

Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. From there it comes in under doors, through window wells and across garage floors. Because surface water carries dirt and chemicals from the ground it passed over, it is handled as unsanitary. Homes across Los Angeles can see this in any season with a strong enough storm.

Crawlspace: The space beneath a raised floor is easy to forget, which lets dampness go unchecked for years. Humidity stays high down there when a pipe drips, the ground cover is damaged or water pools after rain. Over time that leads to sagging insulation, musty odors indoors and soft or cupped flooring. We pump out what has collected, dry the joists and subfloor, and show you where the moisture is coming from.

Condensate: Cooling equipment produces a steady stream of condensation whenever it runs in damp weather. It is meant to exit through a narrow drain tube, and that tube plugs up with slime and debris. A blockage makes the drip pan spill over, wetting the ceiling or floor near the indoor unit. Most owners learn of it from a ceiling stain or drooping drywall, so have the drain line cleared once a year.
